# Mindset: rejection, slumps, nerves, burnout, motivation

Open this file for rejection, a slump, call reluctance, confidence, burnout, motivation or goal setting.
Confidence mostly follows results and burnout mostly follows the job, so most fixes here are reps, metrics
and role design, not pep talks. Numbers: `benchmarks.md`. Excluded claims: `claims-audit.md`.

## Rules the model gets wrong

- Build confidence through reps and small wins, not pep talks: within-person self-efficacy predicts later performance at only ρ = .06 once trajectory is controlled; past performance predicts self-efficacy at .30 (Sitzmann & Yeo 2013). [study]
- Treat burnout as organizational drag first: 89% of sellers report burnout and low-drag sellers hit 1.7× the quota attainment of high-drag ones (Gartner 2022, 908 sellers); fix role clarity and workload before mindset. [data]
- Coach the explanation of a loss, not the feeling: reps attributing an outcome to effort or strategy intend different next actions than those citing ability or luck (Dixon, Spiro & Jamil 2001, 228 reps). [study]
- Optimistic explanatory style predicted 37% more sales and twice the retention in one insurer (Seligman & Schulman 1986, 94 + 103 agents; small, critiqued): use it as a coachable habit, not a hiring screen. [study]
- Name call reluctance as sales call anxiety: fear of negative customer evaluation plus avoidance of specific selling actions, which lowered performance in 189 mortgage salespeople (Verbeke & Bagozzi 2000); treat the avoidance. [study]
- Spend the training hour on skill reps, not grit or growth-mindset sessions: grit correlates .18 with performance and duplicates conscientiousness (Credé 2017); growth-mindset interventions average d = 0.08 (Sisk 2018). [study]
- Plan with mental contrasting, not positive fantasy: people who fantasized more about an outcome achieved less of it, because imagining it attained relaxes effort (Oettingen 2014); overprescribed goals also drove misreporting (Ordóñez 2009). [study]

## In a slump

- Check the inputs before the belief: lead source, territory, a product or support change; "cold streaks come from lost belief, not skill" is unsupported, since belief mostly follows performance (Sitzmann & Yeo 2013). [study]
- Re-watch recordings from a hot streak (pauses, how and when you asked) before changing the script; the tape is evidence and returns flow faster than self-questioning. [practitioner]
- Shrink the unit of success to something fully in your control (a problem question in every call) and count it daily; past performance drives self-efficacy at .30 (Sitzmann & Yeo 2013). [study]
- Take the free reps: practise the fix in role-play or on the lowest-value leads before the next high-stakes call; Farrokh and Cegelski report 50 simulated calls an hour against about 43 real conversations a month. [practitioner]
- Give advice to a peer in the same slump; when struggling, giving advice to someone in the same situation lifts motivation more than receiving it (Eskreis-Winkler 2018). [study]
- Build pipeline rhythm before working on posture: a weekly leads, appointments, presentations, sales count (LAPS) shows the next lead will come; the "with or without you" stance follows the numbers. [practitioner]

## Nerves before a call, or not making the calls

- Classify first: nerves before a call, or avoidance of specific actions (not dialing, skipping the price question, not asking for the next step); the avoidance is what costs performance (Verbeke & Bagozzi 2000). [study]
- Fix attention on the next concrete step (the opener, the first question) rather than on how you are being judged; task concentration and perseverance were the coping strategies studied (Belschak, Verbeke & Bagozzi 2006). [study]
- Say "I am excited" if it helps how you feel; expect no change in how the buyer rates you, since the replication found no observer-rated difference (Brooks 2014; Poynter 2025). [study; mixed replication]
- Name the feeling in one phrase and take one breath; labeling and reappraisal reduce negative feelings in experiments, with small to moderate effects (Webb 2012). [study]
- Use a physical routine before the meeting (a walk, counting backward, a tongue twister); most reps in one B2B study used one to focus on the customer as a person (Kaski 2017, 14 interviews). [study]
- Drill the opener until it needs no thought: Orlob reports new behaviours take five or six tries to feel comfortable, and anxiety drops when the first 20 seconds are automatic. [practitioner]

## After a lost deal: the attribution script

- Run the six attributions in order: effort, strategy, ability, task difficulty, organizational support, luck (Dixon, Spiro & Jamil 2001); land on the most specific controllable one, usually strategy. [study]
- Write the loss in the task frame: feedback aimed at the task helps, feedback aimed at the self is what backfires about a third of the time (Kluger & DeNisi 1996). [study]
- Coach the attribution explicitly for rookies and check it against data for veterans; newcomers respond to failure differently from veterans (296 rookie reps, Dixon follow-up). [study]
- Log lead quality one-on-one as data for marketing; blaming leads on a group call is the habit that separates weak reps from strong ones. [practitioner]

## A rep is burning out

- Diagnose the mismatch, not the person, across six areas: workload, control, reward, community, fairness, values (Maslach & Leiter); cynicism, not tiredness, is the hallmark. [study]
- Change the stressor before offering coping: a break only helps if the stressors change, otherwise they restart; "do more with less" is the definition of job-person mismatch. [practitioner]
- Subtract before adding: 72% of sellers feel overwhelmed by required skills and 50% by technology, and overwhelmed sellers are 45% less likely to attain quota (Gartner 2024, 1,026 sellers). [data]
- Cut role ambiguity in writing (account ownership, stage definitions, decision rights): it is a top performance driver (−.25) and a burnout antecedent (Verbeke 2011; Low 2001). [study]
- Reward beyond pay: a specific thank-you ("you saved us with that client") and a safe-harbor mentor who will not judge were the most requested reliefs. [practitioner]
- Make it a "we" problem: small peer pods that share issues and celebrate wins remove the stigma of confessing burnout. [practitioner]
- Calibrate effort per task (a 9 on the proposal, 6 on the CRM note): the worry-driven side of perfectionism is linked to burnout; no clear link to job performance (Hill & Curran 2016; Harari 2018). [study]
- Default: skip growth-mindset training for performance. Exception: teaching that both skills and the job can be reshaped raised happiness for at least six months (Berg 2023); use it for retention, not quota. [study]

## Motivation for a cold outreach block

- Supply purpose when the task cannot be made interesting: linking a tedious task to an outcome beyond oneself increases persistence (Yeager 2014). [study]
- Turn the block into a test: hold the profile constant and vary one script element, so each call answers a question; curiosity outlasts willpower. [practitioner]
- Time-box: one hour of maximum intensity with brief research per name beats an all-day blitz; start on tier-3 accounts so early flubs cost little. [practitioner]
- Set the daily unit as conversations held, not meetings booked: most activities end in nothing; the activity-to-conversation split is in `references/sales-process-team.md`, section Operating benchmarks for an SDR team. [data]
- WOOP the block: wish (ten conversations today), outcome (two next steps), obstacle ("I stall after the first no"), plan ("if I get a no, I dial the next number within ten seconds"). [practitioner]
- Set learning goals, not only output goals, when the task is new or changing: specific performance goals on complex tasks can hinder learning, and stretch targets shift people toward risk and misreporting (Ordóñez 2009). [study]

## Self-talk lines

- Before a call: "This is me feeling anxious. My job on this call is to surface one problem and let them decide. Opener, first question, then listen." [practitioner]
- After a loss: "We lost because I presented before they named the cost of the problem, not because I can't sell. Next time, no solution until they state an explicit need." [practitioner]
- Manager to a burning-out rep: "Which one thing in this job, if it stopped tomorrow, would give you the most back? We cut that first, then we talk about targets." [practitioner]

## Common mistakes and replacements

- Pep talk after losses → one specific, controllable attribution and one drilled fix (Dixon, Spiro & Jamil 2001; Sitzmann & Yeo 2013). [study]
- "Reps burn out from how they feel about themselves" → audit workload, control, reward, fairness and role clarity; attainment differs 1.7× by drag (Gartner 2022). [data]
- Hire or coach for grit → grit added about 1% of retention variance beyond tenure and is conscientiousness under another name (Eskreis-Winkler 2014; Credé 2017). [study]
- Growth-mindset workshop as the fix → d = 0.08, near zero for adults (Sisk 2018); spend the hour on skill reps. [study]
- "Think positive about the number" → mental contrasting with the obstacle and an if-then plan (Oettingen 2014). [study]
- Stretch quota as motivation → learning goals on complex tasks; targets just out of reach drive misreporting (Ordóñez 2009). [study]
- "I'm just not a phone person" → sales call anxiety is specific avoidance; drill the avoided action in role-play until automatic (Verbeke & Bagozzi 2000). [study]
