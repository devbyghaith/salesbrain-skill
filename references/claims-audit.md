# Claims audit: what the sales corpus gets wrong, and the wording that is safe

This file is the do-not-cite list and the safe-statement table for every statistic and study checked in the sales corpus: 41 claim records from the video notes plus the persuasion-literature blocklist.
Open it before stating any number or study in any deliverable; it overrides `sources.md` and every topic file.
The rule: use the qualitative version of a claim unless a corrected number appears here.
IDs (S = sales statistics, G = Adam Grant citations, C = communication claims) trace to the audit notes; "—" marks items from the persuasion-literature review with no corpus ID.
Verdicts: verified, overstated, misattributed, failed replication, retracted, unverifiable; "do not use" means no safe version exists.

## Speakers' self-reported business figures

- Mistake: stating a speaker's close rate, show rate or revenue as fact. Do this instead: "<Name> reports…", only when a rule depends on the figure; otherwise cut it. [practitioner]
- This covers the 35 unchecked lines: Hormozi's 18 portfolio figures (referrals "close at 80–90%", 36:1 return on ad spend, a 70% show-rate benchmark, a show rate raised from 74% to 91%). [practitioner]
- Also covered: Priestley's team figures ($400k per salesperson; 90,000 assessment takers), Chris Do's revenue, rates and channel statistics, and his course-cost estimates. [practitioner]
- The arithmetic checks pass: the upsell math (20% × $5,000 adds $1,000 to a $1,000 ticket) and the $500-to-$1,500 profit example, which assumes no customers are lost at the higher price. [practitioner]

| Speaker figure | Outside evidence | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| Hormozi reports referrals "close at 80–90%" | Referred customers were worth at least 16% more in a bank panel, nothing like an 80–90% close rate ([Schmitt 2011](https://doi.org/10.1509/jm.75.1.46)) | "Referred customers are more valuable; Hormozi reports much higher close rates in his own portfolio" |
| Chris Do reports a client's close rate rising from 20% to 75% after three coaching sessions | Implausible as a general claim; coaching effects depend on coach skill ([Dahling 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/peps.12123)) | "Coaching can lift close rates; the size depends on the coach. Chris Do reports one client moving from 20% to 75%" |
| Corpus claim: availability is the strongest predictor of sales | Across 268 studies the strongest predictors are selling knowledge, adaptiveness, role clarity, cognitive ability and engagement; availability holds at best in high-velocity selling ([Verbeke 2011](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/20379/ERS-2010-031-ORG.pdf)) | "In high-velocity inbound selling, speed and availability matter; in complex selling, knowledge and adaptiveness predict performance" |

## Corpus principles the evidence contradicts

| Principle, as a user may quote it | What the evidence says | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| Open a cold call with "did I catch you at a bad time?" (Jeremy Miner) | Lowest-scoring opener: 0.9% vs a 1.5% baseline in 2018, and lowest again at 2.15% across 300M+ calls in 2024 ([Gong 2018](https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-opening-lines); [Gong 2024](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/the-best-and-worst-cold-call-openers-backed-by-data-from-300m-calls/)) | State a reason for the call or use a permission-based opener |
| "How are you?" lowers trust on a cold call (Jeremy Miner) | "How are you?" booked meetings at 5.2% vs a 1.5% baseline ([Gong 2018](https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-opening-lines)) | Brief, genuine pleasantries are fine on cold calls |
| The prospect should talk 90% of the time (Cliff Ennico) | No large-sample support; best ratios run 43:57 (2016) to 57% rep talk in won deals (2025) ([Gong 2025](https://www.gong.io/blog/talk-to-listen-conversion-ratio)) | Avoid monologues over 76 seconds; treat talk ratio as a diagnostic |
| Cold calling fails for everyone (Cliff Ennico) | Two-thirds of B2B buyers accept cold calls ([RAIN](https://www.rainsalestraining.com/hubfs/PDFs/Top%20Performance%20in%20Sales%20Prospecting.pdf)) | Cold calling works in B2B when targeted and well opened |
| Break stalls with pain, urgency and fear (Hormozi) | Fear makes indecisive buyers more indecisive 84% of the time, 2.5M conversations ([Dixon](https://customerthink.com/the-problem-of-customer-indecision-and-how-to-get-over-it-interview-with-matt-dixon/)) | Diagnose indecision; recommend, limit options, de-risk |
| Fire a dud within 14 days (Hormozi) | B2B SDR ramp averages 3.0 months ([Bridge Group 2025](https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group)) | Judge ramp against role and deal cycle |
| Cold streaks come from lost belief; burnout from how reps feel about themselves (Hormozi) | Belief mostly follows performance; burnout tracks organisational drag ([Sitzmann & Yeo](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/meta-analytic-investigation-the-within-person-self-efficacy-domain-self-efficacy-product-past-performance-driver-future-performance/10010154485); [Gartner 2022](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2022-08-30-gartner-sales-survey-finds-nearly-90-percent-of-selle)) | Rebuild confidence through skill reps; fix role clarity and workload |
| The strongest driver of behaviour is the need to be consistent (Tony Robbins via Chris Do) | Foot-in-the-door failed a direct replication; the 39-lab dissonance test failed ([Gamian-Wilk & Doliński 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/0033294119872208); [Vaidis 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/25152459231213375)) | Present consistency tactics as weak evidence; ask buyers to generate their own reasons |
| Facts alone lose two-thirds of the audience (Kane via Chris Do) | Statistics move beliefs and attitudes more; stories move intention ([Zebregs 2015](https://doi.org/10.1080/10410236.2013.842528)) | Quantify the problem, then tell the customer story |
| People buy on emotion, not facts (Chris Do) | Argument quality matters more under careful processing, the high-ticket B2B case ([Carpenter 2015](https://doi.org/10.1111/hcre.12054)) | Lead high-stakes deals with a strong business case |
| A loss feels two to three times as strong as a gain, so stress what the prospect stands to lose (cold-email host) | Loss aversion averages λ ≈ 1.96 across 607 estimates ([Brown 2024](https://osf.io/hnefr/)), lower with domain knowledge ([Mrkva 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcpy.1156)); no general loss-frame advantage across 165 effects ([O'Keefe & Jensen 2006](http://www.dokeefe.net/pub/OKeefeJensen06AICA.pdf)) | Use the buyer's own frame; make the risk specific rather than loss-framed |

## Blocklisted authors and sources

- Guéguen (flowers, touch, mimicry, "but you are free" origin): if cited, say he has ten retractions and eight expressions of concern, cite none of it, offer a replicated alternative ([Retraction Watch 2025](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/03/03/nicolas-gueguen-five-studies-bust-size-hitchhiking-retracted/); [database](https://retractiondatabase.org/)). [data]
- Ariely–Gino "sign at the top" (PNAS 2012): if cited, say it is retracted for fabricated field data and the authors' own five replications failed ([PNAS record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458378/); [Data Colada 98](https://datacolada.org/98); [Kristal 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1911695117)). [data]
- Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow chapter 4 (money, eyes, facial-feedback priming): if cited, say its studies score R-index 14 and Kahneman conceded over-trusting them; keep System 1/2 as vocabulary ([Schimmack 2017](https://replicationindex.com/2017/02/02/reconstruction-of-a-train-wreck-how-priming-research-went-of-the-rails/); [Kahneman 2017](https://replicationindex.com/2017/02/02/reconstruction-of-a-train-wreck-how-priming-research-went-of-the-rails/comment-page-1/)). [data]
- Pre-Suasion priming chapters (flag, polling places, backgrounds, flowers): if cited, say the examples failed replication or come from a flagged author; keep "attention sets the agenda" as opinion ([Klein 2014](https://doi.org/10.1027/1864-9335/a000178); [Oakley review](https://barbaraoakley.com/pre-suasion/)). [data]
- LinkedIn SSI post (51% more likely to exceed quota, 45% more opportunities, promoted 17 months faster): if cited, say it is a 2014 marketing post with no method; "LinkedIn reports…" at most ([LinkedIn 2014](https://www.linkedin.com/business/sales/blog/modern-selling/what-does-a-rising-social-selling-index-mean-for-sales-teams)). [data]
- Play Bigger "category kings take 76% of market value": if cited, say the method is unpublished; use pioneer-failure evidence instead, almost half of market pioneers fail ([Golder & Tellis 1993](https://doi.org/10.1177/002224379303000203)). [study]
- 7-38-55 and "tone is 90%": if cited, say the split came from 1967 single-word like/dislike studies and its author says it does not apply to everyday communication ([Mehrabian](http://www.kaaj.com/psych/smorder.html); [Lapakko 2015](https://doi.org/10.56816/2471-0032.1000)). [study]
- Triune brain and "croc brain" (Pitch Anything): if cited, say the layered reptile-brain model "has long been discredited among neurobiologists"; use no brain-layer language ([Cesario 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721420917687); [OA copy](https://osf.io/x83dq)). [study]
