# Personal branding: seller reputation, thought leadership, social selling

Open this file for a founder's or seller's reputation, thought leadership, content meant to generate pipeline, and
social selling. Full source entries live in `benchmarks.md`; quarantined claims in `claims-audit.md`. Under 100 lines,
so no contents table.

## Rules the model usually gets wrong

- Vest the relationship in a seller, not the firm: relationship marketing works best with an individual in B2B (Palmatier 2006, 94-study meta-analysis); only salesperson-owned loyalty drove sales growth (Palmatier 2007, 362 dyads) [study]
- Make social selling pay through thought leadership, not activity: the seller's thought leadership mediates the link from social selling to sales performance (Terho 2022, validated measures) [study]
- Write for hidden buyers who never take a call: 71% have little or no sales contact, 79% favour vendors with consistent quality thought leadership, 57% want quick takeaways (Edelman-LinkedIn 2025, 1,934 buyers, sponsor survey) [data]
- Expect most of the audience to be out-of-market: roughly 5% of B2B buyers are in-market in a given quarter, an estimate its author calls imprecise (Ehrenberg-Bass, 95:5 rule) [data]
- Quote no LinkedIn SSI outcome figures ("51% more likely to exceed quota"), a 2014 vendor blog post with no method or sample; cite the Terho 2022 thought-leadership mediation instead [data]
- Say plainly that attribution lags: no causal study links a seller's posting to pipeline, and buyers shortlist before first contact (see `references/sales-process-team.md`, Planning for how buyers actually buy), so last-touch reports miss the payoff [data]

## Founder-led brand for enterprise deals (complex B2B, committee)

- Put the founder's name on the first cold notes: with no brand or references, the founder is the product, and the market wants the person at the top who knows something it doesn't [practitioner]
- Lead the founder's content and notes with one novel, counter-intuitive market insight ("zero-to-one sales talent doesn't exist"), not "better"; the insight is the brand [practitioner]
- Hand off deliberately: the founder-led ladder runs problem known, solution works, someone pays, many pay, non-founders can sell it; a brand vested only in the founder stalls at the last step [practitioner]
- Build firm loyalty alongside the seller's: relationship-enhancing activity and delivered customer value raise both, and the account otherwise follows the person out of the door (Palmatier 2007) [study]
- Educate the people who position you to buyers (analysts, integrators, podcasters, domain experts); who carries influence differs by market, so map it before posting [practitioner]

## What to post

- Default: short, high-level posts that challenge an assumption (86% want assumption-challenging perspectives, 57% quick takeaways; Edelman-LinkedIn 2025, sponsor survey). Exception: long-form wins on creator channels, not with B2B buyers [data]
- Post problem-led insight, not self-promotion: write trend pieces about the shift your buyers live in, product absent, and show the work itself rather than announcing it [practitioner]
- Operationalize thought leadership as four competencies: operational helpfulness, market foresight, sharing generosity, dialogic responsiveness; recognition as a trusted authority is what mediates payoff (Barry & Gironda 2019, labels from a secondary summary) [study]
- Climb the visible-expert ladder in order: resident expert (clients, colleagues), community or practice-group expert (referrals, speaking), then wider visibility; even level one earns client preference (Hinge "Visible Expert", vendor survey) [practitioner]
- Build authority on a framework or small research (case studies, reviews, primary-source facts) that explains a field, so you get cited whenever the topic arises; niche first, widen later [practitioner]
- Use a slight smile in profile and deck photos: a broad smile raised perceived warmth but lowered perceived competence across five studies including Kickstarter field data (Wang 2017) [study]

## Turning content into conversations

- End posts with a keyword call to action ("comment X and I'll send it"), DM the resource, then ask "serious or just curious?" before booking; the second commitment filters browsers [practitioner]
- Run the content funnel as problem, sample, tool: magnify the problem, give step one free, give a checklist, then follow up in DMs with no pressure [practitioner]
- Link every piece of content to a short niche assessment and route every result to a one-to-one appointment; run a live "introduction to X" session twice a month [practitioner]
- Answer emailed questions in public so one reply serves fifty readers, and make it easy for readers to talk to each other (groups, meetups); an audience becomes a community that way [practitioner]
- Reinvest about one in five prospecting hours into places prospects already gather (blogs, groups, people they follow), and review after two months which hours paid [practitioner]
- Turn one expert hour a week into content through a writer with journalist skills: a short ebook, several posts, scheduled social posts, each ending with the ebook for contact details [practitioner]

## Measuring it honestly

- Track what the evidence links to revenue: meeting acceptance on outreach, proposal support in RFPs, inbound referrals and speaking invitations, not follower counts (Edelman-LinkedIn 2025, sponsor survey) [data]
- Judge over at least a purchase cycle: with roughly 5% in-market per quarter, a post reaches mostly future buyers, so compare quarterly cohorts rather than last-touch attribution (Ehrenberg-Bass) [data]
- Audit the brand on three validated dimensions: appeal, differentiation, recognition (personal brand equity scale, Gorbatov 2021, N = 3,273); it predicts career outcomes, not sales [study]
- Pair posting with selling skill: firm training and adaptive selling strengthen social media's payoff in sales (Ladeira 2025 meta-analysis, 49 studies), so content without sales skill returns less [study]
- Report Hinge, Edelman and 6sense figures as self-reported vendor surveys with no independent replication; each sponsor sells what its figures recommend [data]

## Common mistakes and replacements

| Mistake | Do this instead |
|---|---|
| "Post daily and pipeline follows" | Problem-led insight; measure meeting acceptance and RFP support over quarters |
| Citing "SSI leaders are 51% more likely to exceed quota" | Social selling pays through thought leadership (Terho 2022); SSI figures have no method |
| 45-minute videos because a creator channel says long-form wins | Short, assumption-challenging takeaways (57% want quick takeaways, Edelman 2025) |
| Company-page posts about features | A named person's trend pieces about the buyer's shift, product absent |
| "Be authentic" as the whole plan | A framework or small research that explains the field, in a niche first |
| Founder brand with no hand-off plan | Climb the ladder to "non-founders can sell it"; build firm loyalty alongside the seller's (Palmatier 2007) |
| Quoting Hinge fee premiums or a creator's channel stats as fact | "<Name> reports..." at most, flagged as vendor survey or anecdote; prefer to cut |
| A big grin on the profile photo to look friendly | Slight smile; a broad one trades competence for warmth (Wang 2017) |
