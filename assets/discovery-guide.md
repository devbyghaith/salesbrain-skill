# Discovery call guide template

Constraints baked in: 11–14 questions spread through the call, problem and implication questions
outnumber situation questions, no solution before an explicit need, ends with a dated buyer-owned next
step. See `references/discovery.md` and `references/closing.md`.

```
# Discovery: <company> / <name, role> / <date>

## Before the call (do not ask what you can look up)
- Known: <size, market, recent change, likely trigger>
- Hypothesis: <the problem you think they have, in their words>
- Gaps: [NEEDS INPUT] <what only they can tell you>

## Opening (under 2 minutes)
- Reason for the call + agenda + permission question.

## Questions (11–14, in this order of weight)
Situation (1–3, only what research could not answer)
- …
Problem (3–5)
- …
Implication (3–4: what it costs, who else it touches, what happens if unchanged)
- …
Need-payoff (2–3: make them state the explicit need)
- …

## Qualification notes (fill across touches, not in one interrogation)
- Pain in their words:
- Metrics they care about:
- Economic buyer / decision process / paper process:
- Champion test (will they sell internally without you?):
- Competition and status quo:

## Close (before minute 40 of 45)
- Summary in their words → "Did I get that right?"
- Next step: who / what / by when / what if blocked. Confirm in writing.
```

Self-check:

- Count the questions. Over 14 for a C-suite buyer? Cut situation questions.
- Any pitch before an explicit need? Move it after the need-payoff block.
- Is the next step owned by the buyer and dated?
- Append the self-report: assumptions, [NEEDS INPUT] items, evidence level.
